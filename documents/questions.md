# Canary: Market and Commercial Questions
**Research date:** November 14, 2026

## Purpose
This is an early market-positioning answer based on Canary's current project concept: curated AI threat monitoring, organization-specific relevance, source-backed alerts, and step-by-step mitigation/contingency guidance. Pricing and advertising figures below are hypotheses to validate with customer interviews and pilots, not established Canary prices or guarantees.

## 1. Who has purchasing authority for this product?

**Answer:** The likely budget owner is the person accountable for cybersecurity risk or AI governance. In a small company this is commonly the founder/CEO, CTO, head of engineering, or an outsourced IT/security provider. In a mid-size or large organization it is most likely the CISO/security director, with the CIO/CTO, AI governance lead/Chief AI Officer, risk/compliance, and procurement as influencers or approvers. For a hospital, likely stakeholders include the CISO, CIO, clinical/AI governance leadership, privacy/compliance, and procurement; the exact signer depends on the institution's purchasing thresholds and policies.

Canary should sell first to a champion who feels the problem and can sponsor a pilot: security lead, IT director, engineering leader, or AI governance owner. Identify the economic buyer and procurement process early rather than assuming the champion can sign. Position Canary as a practical, low-friction way to monitor AI-related threats and convert findings into owned actions, not as an autonomous security control.

**Why:** AI security responsibility is distributed. A 2026 report summarized by ITPro says surveyed organizations place AI cybersecurity responsibility across CTO, CISO, CIO, CSO, and CAIO roles; this supports multi-stakeholder selling rather than a single universal buyer. Google's Security Command Center, an adjacent enterprise security product, directs buyers to sales/partners for subscriptions, illustrating that enterprise purchases commonly involve organizational procurement.

## 2. What budget does Canary replace within companies?

**Answer:** Do not claim Canary replaces a single existing line item. It is more credible to position it as consolidating or augmenting:
- Staff time spent manually checking advisories, vendor notices, threat reports, and AI security research.
- A portion of threat-intelligence subscriptions or analyst services, if the customer already buys them.
- Periodic AI security assessments and planning/reporting work, especially where customers repeatedly commission assessments.
- Some risk-management or governance workflow software, only if Canary actually delivers the required controls and evidence.

Canary should not claim to replace a SIEM, EDR, vulnerability scanner, incident-response retainer, compliance platform, or managed security team. These tools detect, enforce, or operate controls that Canary's current concept does not provide. A defensible value case is hours saved and faster, better-traceable triage and planning—not avoided breach costs unless supported by customer-specific evidence.

## 3. How much should customers pay?

**Market evidence:** Public adjacent offerings show a wide range because they solve different problems. TrustTrace lists $49/month Developer, $149/month Pro, $349/month Team, and $799/month Enterprise for AI security scans. Penaxtra lists €290/month Starter and €1,290/month Business for AI security posture management. At the enterprise end, Google's Security Command Center Premium subscription has a $15,000 annual minimum, but it bundles a broader cloud security service and is not a direct Canary comparable. AWS Security Hub lists some partner AI security tools at $130/resource/month with minimum resource counts. These examples are directional anchors, not equivalent products.

**Canary pricing hypothesis (USD):**
- Free or limited pilot: $0 for a time-limited evaluation or limited sources/assets; do not provide ongoing labor-intensive support for free.
- Small team: $50–$200/month, annual equivalent $600–$2,400.
- Growing team: $200–$750/month, annual equivalent $2,400–$9,000, based on monitored inventory, seats, integrations, or service level.
- Larger/regulated organization: $10,000–$30,000+ annually, custom scope and security review; this should wait until Canary can demonstrate enterprise-grade controls, reliability, support, and procurement readiness.

**Recommended first test:** offer 5–10 design partners a paid 60–90 day pilot at $100–$300/month for small teams, and separately quote larger organizations based on scope. Ask what budget they would use and what evidence is needed to renew. Do not set final pricing from competitor list prices alone. Canary should choose one primary pricing metric (for example, organization tier plus monitored AI assets) and avoid unpredictable per-alert billing that penalizes customers for discovering threats.

## 4. How much should advertising cost?

There is no reliable fixed advertising cost for a new product without knowing geography, target audience, channel, conversion rate, and deal size. Keep initial paid acquisition small and measurable.

**Recommendation:** allocate $500–$2,000/month for an initial 6–8 week test after the landing page, demo, and tracking are ready. Focus on one or two channels and a narrow audience (e.g., small software companies adopting LLMs; security/AI governance leads). Treat this as an experiment, not a permanent budget. Track qualified demo requests, pilot starts, and customer acquisition cost, not impressions alone. Pause or adjust if the leads do not match the target buyer.

For search ads, broad averages are weak predictors: WordStream's 2025 cross-industry Google Ads average CPC was $5.26, while B2B tech benchmarks differ by dataset and keyword. Competitive cybersecurity terms can be substantially more expensive. At $500–$2,000/month, a $10–$30 CPC would buy roughly 17–200 clicks before conversion; this is arithmetic for planning, not a forecast. Early outreach, founder-led demos, relevant communities, partner/MSP referrals, and educational content may produce better learning per dollar than broad paid advertising.

## 5. What prevents a cybersecurity vendor from adding the same functionality?

Nothing guarantees they cannot. The market already has adjacent and overlapping functionality. Microsoft describes AI Security Posture Management within Defender for Cloud; Google Security Command Center includes AI security/posture management; AWS Security Hub lists AI security partners such as Noma, Oligo, and Zenity. Specialist vendors also advertise AI security posture assessment, scanning, governance, runtime protection, and reporting. Canary should not claim the feature category is uncontested or technically impossible to copy.

**Potential advantages to build and validate:**
- A genuinely useful cross-vendor source set, with reliable ingestion, provenance, dates, deduplication, and transparent uncertainty.
- High-quality matching between threats and the customer's actual AI inventory, not generic AI news summaries.
- A consistent workflow from evidence-backed alert to ordered mitigation guide, owner, review, and plan history.
- Trustworthy customer-specific history and integrations that reduce setup and operational friction.
- A focused underserved segment (for example small teams without a security analyst) and a product experience that enterprise suites do not prioritize.
- Demonstrated signal quality, response-time usefulness, and measurable reduction in manual triage work.

These are potential advantages to build and validate, not current moats. Large security vendors have distribution, customer relationships, telemetry, and bundling advantages. Canary should differentiate through focus, source transparency, actionable guidance, and cross-platform coverage, while remaining compatible with existing security tools. Validate through customer interviews which gap existing vendors leave unmet and whether buyers will pay separately for it.

## Limitations and next validation
- Competitor feature descriptions and prices change; verify before using in a pitch or financial model.
- Public prices are not directly comparable: some products scan code or AI assets, others secure cloud infrastructure or provide runtime controls.
- The recommended Canary prices and advertising budget are strategic estimates, not market facts. Validate willingness to pay with target buyers and actual pilot conversion.
- Purchasing authority varies by company size, industry, procurement policy, and contract value.
